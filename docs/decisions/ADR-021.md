# ADR-021: Keyword search returns pages that hold most of the query's words, stemmed

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-09-12
Relates to: ADR-005, PRD sections 7 and 10

## Context

Keyword search matched any one word of the query. Every question returned pages, including questions Cairn has no answer to: "which ESC firmware did I flash on the 5 inch build" found five peptide pages through words like "which" and "flash". An agent cannot tell that noise from an answer, and the first thing a new user would try shows it.

The eval could not see the problem. It scored only recall@5, which rewards returning something for everything, and the two queries with no answer were unscored.

Found while fixing it:

1. Requiring most of the words, without stemming, lost real answers: the MT-2 page says "peptides", so "tanning peptide" missed it.
2. Treating words found on more than half the pages as optional (tried as a fix for 1) made a rare, vague word such as "help" required instead, and lost the sleep peptides for "peptides that help with sleep". With stemming in place it was not needed, and it was removed.
3. Search returns chunks. Once pages that hold every word rank first, all of one page's chunks can fill the result and hide the next page.

## Decision

1. **Filler words do not count.** A query is reduced to its distinct terms without function words, using short lists for English, Italian and Dutch. A query made only of function words keeps them.
2. **A page must hold most of the terms**: all of them for one or two terms, more than half for three or more. The terms may be in different sections of the page. Pages that hold more of them rank first, then by BM25.
3. **Each matching page's best chunk comes first,** and a page's other chunks follow once every matching page has one.
4. **Rules 1 to 3 live in core** (`packages/core/src/search/terms.ts`) and in the search conformance suite, so every backend applies them the same way.
5. **The SQLite index stems with FTS5's Porter tokenizer** (`porter unicode61 remove_diacritics 2`), so "peptides" finds "peptide" and "combine" finds "combined". Porter is English. On other languages it changes little, and query and text are stemmed alike, so it never stops a word from matching itself. Which page holds which term is asked of FTS5, so the rule agrees with the stemmer exactly.
6. **An index built with another tokenizer is recreated at startup,** and rebuilt from the pages before the server answers (chunks are derived data, ADR-005). The port says so with `needsRebuild`.
7. **The eval scores questions with no answer.** A query marked `expected: none` is right when search returns nothing, reported beside recall@5 as "no answer". Six such queries are in `eval/queries.yaml`, several sharing one or two words with the wiki on purpose.

## Consequences

1. Before and after, SQLite FTS5, keyword mode, on the 96-page peptide wiki: recall@5 1.00 on 14 queries before and after; no-answer queries returning nothing, 0 of 6 before and 6 of 6 after. Nasal spray for anxiety moved from rank 2 to rank 1, tendon healing from rank 2 to rank 3.
2. A query in the text's own words does best. When the text uses other words ("stack" for "combine" on the tirzepatide page), a two-word query can return pages that mention both words rather than the page meant. The server instructions, tool description and skill tell agents to use two or three distinctive words and to retry with synonyms when nothing comes back.
3. A query of one or two words must find all of them. That is the trade for returning nothing when nothing is there.
4. All the eval queries so far were written by the agent that made this change, from the same wiki. The numbers are a floor on overfitting, not evidence against it, until the owner's queries are in.
5. Upgrading an existing database rebuilds its index once, at the first start: 96 pages in 61 ms here.
6. Italian and Dutch are handled only by the stop-word lists. A stemmer per language, if Italian or Dutch notes arrive, is a later decision with its own eval queries.
